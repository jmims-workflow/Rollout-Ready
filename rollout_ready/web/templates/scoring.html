How it works

A 2 minute read on what Rollout Ready does, how it scores, and what each answer means.

  1. What the app does

    When you change AI instructions your team relies on, Rollout Ready runs the version you're trying on saved examples and compares it with the version your team uses now. You review the evidence, update your team's tool yourself, then confirm, so everyone knows which version is in use.

    An example is only an input: what someone on your team would paste in. Nobody writes the ideal answer.

  2. How the AI reviewer scores

    The AI reviewer is Claude. It reads one answer at a time and scores it from 1 to 5 on each quality in what good looks like, with a one-sentence reason for every score. It never sees which version wrote the answer, or the other version's answer, so it can't take sides.

    Quick checks run too: plain rules like a word limit, which always give the same answer.

  3. Why one broken example matters more than the average

    A change can lift the average while quietly breaking the examples you care about. In the demo, version 3 of the Sales follow-up assistant raised the average from 3.6 to 3.9, but two emails stopped answering the customer's questions. So the answer is "Not ready to roll out."

    An example got worse if its score dropped by half a point or more, it fails a quick check it used to pass, or it newly misses a deal-breaker. It got better if its score rose by half a point or more, or it passes a quick check it used to fail. Anything smaller is about the same, because every run varies a little.

    Six to eight good examples catch most breakages. They can't prove a version will never break, because your team will paste in things nobody thought to test. That's why every result says how many examples it is based on.

  4. What each verdict means

    • Ready to roll out: nothing got worse, at least one got better, and no deal-breaker is missed.
    • Needs review: some got worse, more got better, and no deal-breaker is missed. Look at the ones that got worse before deciding.
    • Not ready to roll out: a deal-breaker is missed on any example, or some got worse and no more got better.
    • No clear difference: nothing got clearly better or worse.
    • Test incomplete: an answer or review is missing or was cut off, so there's no verdict until it runs.

More detail

Deal-breakers

Some qualities matter more than the rest: getting the facts right, or answering every question. Mark those as deal-breakers. If the AI reviewer scores a deal-breaker {{ cutoff }} or lower on any example, even once when an example runs more than once, the version you're trying is "Not ready to roll out", whatever the average says.

Most sets of AI instructions have one or two deal-breakers. If everything is a deal-breaker, nothing is.

Disagreeing with the AI reviewer

The AI reviewer is a model, and it can be wrong. On any example, choose "I disagree with the AI reviewer", say what your call is, and give a one-line reason. Your call changes how that example counts for those results only, and the answer is worked out again.

The AI reviewer's scores are never changed, a missed deal-breaker still blocks a rollout, and the result says which examples you overrode and why. If the examples or what good looks like change, your call doesn't carry over: you decide again.

What rolling out does and doesn't do

Putting a version into use records your decision: which version, when, who confirmed it, and the test it relied on. You copy the instructions into your team's tool yourself, then confirm.

Rollout Ready doesn't update your AI tool, and it doesn't watch it afterward. "In use" means the version the owner last confirmed.

What happens to your saved work
  • This demo, with sample results: what you change lives in your browser until you close it. Nothing is saved on the server.
  • The live demo, which runs on Claude: your tests are private to you and last up to 24 hours, or until you close this browser. They may reset sooner.
  • Run on your own computer with a Claude key: tests, examples and every rollout are saved on that computer.
